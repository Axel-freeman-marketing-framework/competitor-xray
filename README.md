### The gap this fills

Every competitor-analysis skill produces **analysis**: SWOT tables, feature matrices, positioning maps. Pretty reports nobody acts on. This skill produces a **copy-list** — concrete, ranked items a founder can start testing this week, because a competitor already proved they work.

### See it work

📄 **[View the demo transcript](examples/demo-transcript.md)** — one competitor name in, a ranked copy-list out.

### The problem

Founders ask "should I do what they do?" and get a 20-tab research project. Or worse — a consultant's SWOT. What they need is judgment: *which 5 elements of the competitor's playbook are worth borrowing, and which one to run first.*

### The method

Free channels only, in order of reliability:

1. **Website** — offer, promise, pricing, proof
2. **Their emails** — subscribe, record frequency/hooks/CTAs
3. **Ad libraries** — Meta/Google/LinkedIn/TikTok, free, show what they pay to repeat
4. **Social cadence** — what formats earn engagement
5. **Review mining** — G2/Capterra/Reddit: complaints = your positioning
6. **Job boards** — open roles reveal strategy
7. **Change detection** — landing-page edits = live experiments

Paid databases are optional accelerators. The x-ray works with zero budget.

### The output

1. **3 competitors** — one line each on why they're winning
2. **The seams** — what they all avoid, what users complain about
3. **Copy-list** — 5–10 items, ranked by speed-to-test
4. **This week's move** — the single item to run first

### Who it's for

- **Founders & solo operators** who need competitive judgment, not a research project
- **Indie hackers** shipping a SaaS into an existing market
- **Freelancers & agencies** positioning against established players
- **Marketers** who want competitor research tied to weekly execution

### A note on "copying"

Copying here means legal competitive positioning — studying public pages, ads, and reviews, then adapting proven elements to your product. Not plagiarism, not scraping private data, not fake reviews.
